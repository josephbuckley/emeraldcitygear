The offseason didn't bother waiting for the offseason. Hours after the Mariners closed out a 76-86 season with a 7-3 win over the Angels on Sunday, Dan Wilson stood in front of the cameras and told Seattle he won't be managing the club in 2027. He'd told the players before the finale. By the evening, the team made it official: after two years, 187 wins, and the second-best winning percentage in franchise history, the Dan Wilson era is over.

Let's be honest about what this is, because the reporting is a mess on one point: nobody has said clearly whether Wilson was fired or stepped down. The New York Times reported a firing; Yahoo reported Wilson informed the club he wouldn't return; Wilson himself didn't specify in his postgame presser. What matters is the outcome — and chairman John Stanton's telling addendum that the organization wants to keep Wilson around in another role. The Hall of Fame catcher isn't being exiled. He's being moved.

And that stings in a specific way, because Dan Wilson the Mariner is unimpeachable. Catcher here from 1994 to 2005. Took over on Aug. 22, 2024, the day Scott Servais was fired, and steadied a freefall into a 21-13 finish. Then 2025: the first AL West title in 24 years and a run to Game 7 of the ALCS — one win from the first World Series in franchise history. That banner is real, and it's his.

But 2026 is also real. A season that began with World Series aspirations ended ten-plus games under .500, and the numbers are brutal in a way that ends managerial tenures: Cal Raleigh followed a 60-homer, MVP-runner-up season by hitting .182 with 23 homers. The team scored 664 runs — third-fewest in the majors. The offense fell from 9th in runs to 28th; the staff ERA from 13th to 20th. Luis Castillo was dealt to the White Sox in August. Colt Emerson's debut year ended with wrist surgery. Multiple reports during the season cited a lack of accountability in the clubhouse as a theme.

You can argue — fairly — that no manager survives his best players all collapsing at once. You can also look at 76-86 with this core and conclude a new voice was the only move left. Both things are true.

So what comes next? Jerry Dipoto and Justin Hollander hold their end-of-season press conference today, and the managerial search officially opens with it. Seattle should be an attractive job: Gilbert and Woo front a real rotation, the young core is under control, and the 2025 run proved the window is real. But the next manager inherits the same questions that sank this season — how to fix an offense that forgot how to hit, how to hold a young clubhouse accountable, and how much say they'll get over a coaching staff where pitching coaches Pete Woodworth and Trent Blank are under contract and expected to stay.

One name is already in the mix. Bob Nightengale reports Torey Lovullo "already may be the leading candidate." The fit is easy to see: ten years in Arizona, a pennant in 2023, and his contract is up after an 86-win season that came up one step short of the Wild Card. But treat it as what it is — Nightengale reading the tea leaves on press-conference morning, not a hire. Today's presser decides what this rumor is worth: if Dipoto says the search is wide open, it's a fun what-if; if he won't rule anything out, Lovullo just became the story of the week.

The early speculative lists are already floating the usual names — Bob Melvin, Brandon Hyde, Rocco Baldelli among them — but nothing has been reported on actual candidates yet. This will be the third dugout change in four years. Dipoto can't afford to get this one wrong.

Thank you, Dan. The 2025 division title will hang forever, and no 76-86 season takes it down. But the standard is the standard — and now the most important hire of the Dipoto era is sitting on his desk.
