The starter trade happened — and that was the last one. A day after the Mariners sent Bryce Miller, Logan Evans and Jose A. Ferrer to Kansas City for Maikel Garcia, Jerry Dipoto told the beat Wednesday that the likelihood of trading another starting pitcher "just went from very high to zero," per MLB Trade Rumors' Steve Adams, citing Adam Jude of the Seattle Times. The full quote, as carried by Roundtable's Brady Farkas: "Anything that was possible for us yesterday remains possible for us today. With the exception being that the likelihood of us trading a starter just went from very high to zero."

That closes the loop this blog has been tracking all week. On Saturday this post's predecessor said Dipoto was eight-deep and didn't need to trade a starter; on Friday, ESPN's Alden Gonzalez reported the Mariners were expected to move at least one. Both were true in sequence: the move happened, it was Miller, and the door is now shut. The rotation lines up as Logan Gilbert, George Kirby, Bryan Woo, Emerson Hancock and touted prospect Kade Anderson, per MLB Trade Rumors — with Ryan Sloan as the next arm up — a top prospect Dipoto said could arrive "at any point" next season, per Adam Jude.

Dipoto also said the Garcia deal "didn't ding our payroll flexibility in any way." He put the rest plainly, per Ryan Divish of the Seattle Times as summarized by SoDo Mojo: "Our position-player group is not done," with a right-handed bat "ideal" and a want to get "aggressive." And he named what's still on the list in the same breath: another right-handed bat, a veteran infield presence, and — with Ferrer gone to Kansas City — an established left-handed reliever. That last one is the sharpest edge of the trade's cost, and it's the item to watch first: Dipoto said at the GM meetings he wants "one, maybe two leverage relievers," and the Ferrer flip means the bullpen need is now bigger than the one he walked into Vegas with.

The bat, though, is where the news got worse for Seattle on the same day. Jeff Passan reported Wednesday that the Padres hosted Seiya Suzuki for a visit on Monday — and that San Diego "could be the favorites" to sign him, per NBC Sports' write-up of Passan's ESPN report. The Padres missed out on Suzuki when he came over from Japan four years ago; this time they hosted him first. Suzuki, 32, declined the Cubs' $23.15 million qualifying offer, so signing him still costs Seattle a draft pick, and he is coming off a .270/.368/.473 season with 26 home runs. The Mariners are still in the pursuit, and the Seattle visit is firmer than it looked a day ago: Divish reports the Mariners "plan to bring him to Seattle" for a visit, per SoDo Mojo's summary of his Seattle Times reporting. But the field is wider than San Diego. Divish lists the Giants, Blue Jays and Phillies as suitors, the Red Sox have also been reported hot on Suzuki, and a reunion with the Cubs isn't ruled out. SoDo Mojo figures that crowd could push Suzuki past MLB Trade Rumors' four-year, $105 million projection to something like five years and $120–130 million — that's SoDo Mojo's estimate, not a reported ask — which would make it the biggest free-agent splash of the Stanton–Dipoto years. The chase this blog framed on Saturday as "Suzuki is the test of whether the ceiling bends" now has a frontrunner wearing a different uniform, and the price of bending just went up.

None of this changes what the Garcia trade bought: the left-side infielder off Dipoto's Vegas list, in ink, under contract through 2030. What it does change is the shape of the rest of the winter. The next upgrade can't come from the rotation anymore, and the likeliest free-agent bat now has San Diego in front. If Suzuki slips away, the fallback is the route Dipoto named Wednesday — "other ways to access the players that might finish off our team, be it free agency or other types of trade" — with the CBA expiring December 1, and the signing flurry expected to run from Thanksgiving weekend right up to the deadline — the Winter Meetings, scheduled for December 7–9 in San Diego, fall after it and will likely be cancelled in a lockout.

What to watch: whether Suzuki's Seattle visit happens before the CBA clock runs out, who the left-handed reliever is, and whether Dipoto's "zero" holds when the phones get loud next week.
