The GM meetings opened in Las Vegas on November 9, and Jerry Dipoto walked in with a list instead of a wish. One or two hitters. A bullpen arm. A veteran infield presence. Roughly $30 to $35 million to spend, with trades ahead of free agency in the order of operations. The meetings ran through Thursday, November 12, and for a front office that just finished rebuilding its dugout, this is where the roster half of the reset started.

The telling part is what he put first. In an interview with MLB.com, Dipoto made defense the priority for 2027, saying last season's defense made life hard on a good pitching staff and cost the team outs night after night. If you watched this team boot away innings behind Logan Gilbert and George Kirby, that lands. It also reframes the winter: this is a defense story first and a bat story second. The names floated for the fix — Mauricio Dubón, Matt Chapman, and Joey Ortiz — come from the outlet reporting the interview, not from Dipoto himself, so treat them as the shape of the idea rather than a target list. But the shape is clear: glove-first infield help that doesn't hand outs back.

Then there's the detail that rewrites a piece of the manager search. Ken Rosenthal reported that a select group of veteran clubhouse leaders sat in on the final interviews for Jason Kanzler — this after Dipoto said last month that players would not be asked to be part of the interview process. That report reached most of us second-hand, via Bleacher Report's pickup of Rosenthal's reporting, so hold it at that strength. But if it's right, it's a meaningful tell about how this hire was made: the room Kanzler has to lead got a voice in choosing him, whatever the public line was in October. For a manager whose stated top priority is building player relationships, that's not a bad foundation — it's just not the one we were told about.

On the trade front, be careful to separate ideas from talks, because this week will blur them on purpose. Jon Morosi floated a rental swap built around Logan Gilbert — in his final year of arbitration — for Cleveland outfielder Steven Kwan, who hit .282 last season and fits the left-field need Seattle has named. Floated is the operative word: that's a radio-shape idea, not a reported negotiation. Gilbert and Kirby are the two arms every team in Vegas would ask about, and George Kirby and Emerson Hancock are both names other clubs are expected to bring up this week. Being asked about is different from being on the block, and a front office that just spent a month preaching pitching depth isn't shopping its rotation. Expect the asks. Don't mistake them for offers.

The reunion angle needs the same care. Carlos Santana and Eugenio Suárez both fit the "veteran infield presence" description, and the outlets suggesting them say so plainly — but Dipoto has named no targets, and neither name has been tied to Seattle by actual reporting. Suárez hit 26 home runs for Cincinnati last season and would be a real bat. Santana played only eight major league games, so a return for him would be a clubhouse move, not an offensive one. Either could make sense. Neither is news yet.

Day two of the meetings put a ceiling on the list. The Mariners reportedly aren't comfortable approaching $100 million for a free agent this winter, which turns Dipoto's $30–35 million from a wish list into a budget. That rules out the big-ticket names: a long-term Randy Arozarena return is off the table, and the club has told J.P. Crawford it plans to move on, with Colt Emerson as the shortstop of the future. That ceiling comes via Sodo Mojo's summary of Adam Jude's Seattle Times reporting, backed by a separate Yardbarker write-up — the Times piece itself is paywalled, so treat it as reported, not directly verified.

Then the ceiling got its first test. Overnight, Jude and Ryan Divish reported — via MLB Trade Rumors — that the Mariners do have interest in Seiya Suzuki, the 32-year-old Cubs right fielder entering free agency. MLB Trade Rumors projects him at four years and $105 million, just above the reported line. Suzuki is the exception being tested: he fits as a right-handed bat to share right field and the designated hitter spot with Dominic Canzone, and Dipoto has talked openly about getting Seattle back into the Japanese player market after a dry spell stretching back to Yusei Kikuchi's departure after 2021. The counterweight is John Stanton's September preference for signing players earlier in their careers. The other flip from yesterday: Teruaki Sato, the one possible splash left standing, is one Seattle is unlikely to bid on — not on price but on fit, with the club wanting right-handed bats, per Spokesman-Review reporting carried by NPB Wire. So the ceiling is still the story, and Suzuki is the test of whether it bends. Believe the interest when you see a meeting or an offer.

Day three brought a whiplash instead of a name that fits. Jon Morosi of MLB Network reported Wednesday that the Mariners are interested in free agent Ian Happ, the 32-year-old Cubs left fielder and four-time Gold Glove winner — a second Chicago outfielder on Dipoto's radar, and one with a connection to Kanzler from their shared time in the Cubs organization. MLB Trade Rumors projects Happ at three years and $45 million, which would sit inside the Mariners' free-agent wheelhouse where Suzuki's $105 million does not. But within about five hours the same day, Adam Jude downplayed the Happ connection, with MLB Trade Rumors' Mariners notes calling Happ — like Teruaki Sato — an unlikely fit in Seattle. Both reports can be true: Seattle may have checked in, then cooled. Trust the local beat here, and the baseball case backs it up — Happ struck out 29.7% of the time in 2026, exactly the profile this front office is trying to move away from, and his Gold Gloves owe more to his arm than his range, at minus-4 Outs Above Average in left over the last five seasons. The Kanzler connection is thin, too — Kanzler worked the prospect side in Chicago. So Happ is not the realistic version of this winter's outfield search after all. Suzuki is the free agent to chase hard, and the next serious name is more likely to come through a trade.

The bullpen half of the list got sharper on day three, too. Dipoto said Tuesday he wants "one, maybe two leverage relievers, and if they're righty or lefty, doesn't matter to us" — via Adam Jude and Ryan Divish at the Seattle Times — and Morosi reported Wednesday that Seattle is among the teams engaged in the left-handed relief market, alongside Philadelphia, Washington, Kansas City, and St. Louis. And the Arozarena door closed a little further: his agent Scott Boras is asking for somewhere around six years and at least $150 million, per Divish and Jude's Wednesday reporting — a number to treat as an opening ask, not a market price, since Boras always aims high and early projections sit closer to four or five years around $100 million. Either way, it is not a number Seattle is going to meet, and it pairs with the East Coast preference that was already in the room. Dipoto also pushed back on the rotation-trade chatter this week, saying the Mariners are eight-deep in starters and don't need to trade one — which tempers the floated Gilbert-for-Kwan idea above a notch further: expect the asks, still don't mistake them for offers.

What we know, then: the list is real, the money is roughly $30–35M, the priority is defense — and the meetings are over. They ended Thursday with only one major move anywhere: Evan Phillips signing with Atlanta for three years and $35 million, which takes him off Seattle's reliever board. The offseason proper has now begun. The Winter Meetings are expected to be canceled with a lockout looming on December 2, and the signing flurry is expected to run from Thanksgiving weekend through December 1, when the CBA expires and the lockout question stops being theoretical. If Dipoto wants his winter done before the sport shuts its doors, that flurry is when it happens.

The dugout got rebuilt first. Now we find out what he builds around it.
