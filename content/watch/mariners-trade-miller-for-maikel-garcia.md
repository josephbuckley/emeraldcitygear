We knew a starter was going. ESPN's Alden Gonzalez reported Friday that the Mariners were expected to move at least one, with George Kirby, Bryce Miller and Emerson Hancock the more attainable arms. Adam Jude had Kirby and Hancock as the most likely. It was Miller.

Seattle is acquiring third baseman Maikel Garcia, right-handed reliever James McArthur and minor-league reliever L.P. Langevin from Kansas City, in a six-player trade first reported by ESPN's Jeff Passan. Ryan Divish of the Seattle Times added Logan Evans and Jose A. Ferrer to the Royals' return. USA Today reported the teams announced the deal Wednesday.

**To Seattle:** Garcia, McArthur, Langevin
**To Kansas City:** Miller, Evans, Ferrer

Garcia is exactly the player type Dipoto described in Vegas, whether or not his name ever came up there: a right-handed bat who plays the left side of the infield, on a team that named defense its 2027 priority. Garcia won the Gold Glove at third base in 2025 and was an All-Star that year, hitting .286/.351/.449 with 16 home runs, 74 RBIs and 23 steals in 160 games. Seattle finished last in the majors in Outs Above Average in 2026, at -57, per SI. Third base needed a real answer, and this is one, under contract through 2030 on the five-year, $57.5 million deal Kansas City gave him last December, with a club option for 2031. He was also the MVP of the 2026 World Baseball Classic, leading Venezuela to the title.

"It is rare to have an opportunity to acquire a player signed for the long-term who fits our needs as well as Maikel does," Mariners President of Baseball Operations Jerry Dipoto said in a statement. "His ability to impact the game with his bat, his defense and his baserunning makes us a more athletic and well-rounded team."

He also fits the contact turn Seattle keeps talking about. Garcia struck out just 50 times in 82 games in 2026 and 84 times in 160 games in 2025. That is the opposite profile from the 29.7% strikeout rate that made Ian Happ an unlikely fit here.

The questions are health and slug. Garcia played only 82 games in 2026, limited by a left-hand issue and a hamstring strain, per SI, and hit .268/.334/.381 with 3 home runs and a 102 OPS+. If Seattle is getting the 2025 version, this is a foundational infield piece for five years. If it's getting the 2026 version, it's a very good glove with a light bat at a spot that still needs production.

And the cost is serious. Miller is not a throw-in: 12-8 with a 2.94 ERA in 2024, sixth in the American League, before injuries and inconsistency in 2025 and 2026 (4-10, 4.17 ERA in 118 2/3 innings this past season). Evans missed all of 2026 after Tommy John surgery but was rotation depth Seattle valued. Ferrer is the sneaky pain — a 26-year-old lefty who appeared in 73 games with a 4.18 ERA for a bullpen that already needed help, flipped one year after Seattle acquired him from Washington. Trading him doesn't just fail to fix the bullpen Dipoto listed in Vegas; it makes that item longer. Seattle had already declined Miller's $6.075 million option, so he was heading for arbitration rather than a guaranteed rotation spot.

Seattle can make this trade because the rotation front is still Logan Gilbert, Bryan Woo and Kirby, with Hancock and top prospects Kade Anderson and Ryan Sloan behind them. That's the eight-deep leverage Dipoto talked about, spent — not on a rental, but on a 26-year-old infielder controlled for five years.

What this doesn't do is replace Randy Arozarena. Garcia is not the everyday power bat for left field, and that hole is still the winter's biggest one, especially now that Seiya Suzuki declined his qualifying offer and would cost a draft pick. One item on Dipoto's Vegas list — the left-side infielder — is checked, in ink. The bat, the bullpen arm, and now a Ferrer replacement are still on it, with the December 1 CBA deadline still doing the counting.
